How Implied differs from a bell curve

01

Everyone else draws a bell curve.

An expected-move tool takes one number, the at-the-money straddle, and turns it into a symmetric range. Same odds up as down. Same shape for every stock.

02

The market doesn't.

Index puts cost more than calls the same distance away. Implied uses every liquid strike, so the downside band stretches further than the upside, exactly as far as the market pays for it.

03

Some names price a moonshot.

For volatile stocks the market charges for extreme rallies. The outer band runs well past where a bell curve says moves become impossible.

04

Before earnings, it can split in two.

When the market prices a jump up or a jump down, the middle becomes the least likely place to land. Implied calls this the Fork: it marks both outcomes on the chart and names the prices.
